# BUY HARD — three-minute screenplay

Film: **You handle today.** One damaged shipment, two purchasing routes, one buyer.

A piano ruins a delivery containing cups and bread. The owner knows how to cover the immediate emergency and delegates replacement supply. Cups are available through an online store; the bakery accepts orders by email. The buyer handles the work appropriate to each supplier and brings back the decisions that need the owner.

## Story rules

- The accident affects the shipment. Show both product types at the opening so the two recovery paths need no extra explanation.
- The owner has chosen to get emergency supplies. Reuse known specifications, quantities, addresses and supplier preferences; ask only for missing information that changes the decision.
- Treat emergency pickup as a plan, not confirmed inventory. Do not assume identical coverage for cups and perishable bread; use known needs and actual delivery requirements for each.
- Do not reorder an unrelated seeded cup draft as if the damaged shipment triggered it. Check existing incoming orders and replacement arrangements before creating duplicate buys.
- Prepare purchases within the applicable rules and obtain required approval before ordering. A combined story does not imply the current interface supports a single approval for both purchases.
- The owner’s later bread-address message illustrates another exception. A requested change is not a supplier-accepted change.
- No technology-provider names in narration, added captions or title cards. No routine stock updates, inbox navigation or status-check chores in the narration.
